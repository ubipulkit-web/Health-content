# 3. Semaglutide for weight loss: how it works and what to consider

Medicines explainer | Evidence, benefits and risk communication

You may have heard of semaglutide through brand names such as Wegovy or Ozempic.

Both contain semaglutide, but they are used for different purposes. Wegovy is licensed for weight management in appropriate people, while Ozempic is primarily used to treat type 2 diabetes.

Semaglutide can help some people lose weight, but it is a prescription medicine rather than a quick solution. It should be used alongside changes to diet, physical activity and other aspects of weight management.

## What is semaglutide?

Semaglutide belongs to a group of medicines called GLP-1 receptor agonists.

GLP-1 is a hormone that your body naturally releases after eating.

Semaglutide acts in a similar way and can:

- reduce appetite

- help you feel full sooner

- help you stay full for longer

- slow the movement of food through your stomach

Together, these effects can reduce how much you feel like eating.

For people with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide can also help regulate blood glucose.

## Is Wegovy the same as Ozempic?

They contain the same active medicine — semaglutide — but they are not interchangeable products.

Wegovy is licensed for weight management in certain people.

Ozempic is used primarily to manage type 2 diabetes.

The doses, licensing and reasons for prescribing them can differ.

You should only use semaglutide that has been prescribed for you and according to the instructions provided by your healthcare professional.

## Who may be offered semaglutide for weight management?

Semaglutide is not appropriate for everyone who wants to lose weight.

NHS eligibility depends on clinical criteria and access through weight-management services.

Healthcare professionals consider factors including:

- your body mass index, or BMI

- health conditions related to your weight

- your previous attempts to manage weight

- whether treatment is clinically appropriate

- any conditions or medicines that could affect its safety

Different BMI thresholds may be used for people from some ethnic backgrounds because the risk of weight-related health problems can occur at lower BMI levels.

Your eligibility should therefore be assessed individually rather than by comparing your weight with someone else's.

## How is semaglutide taken?

Wegovy is usually given as an injection once a week.

Treatment normally starts at a low dose.

The dose is then increased gradually according to the treatment schedule.

This gradual increase helps your body adjust to the medicine and can reduce some side effects.

Do not increase your dose faster than recommended.

## How much weight can I expect to lose?

There is no single amount that everyone will lose.

Clinical trials show that semaglutide can produce meaningful weight loss in many people when combined with lifestyle support, but individual responses vary considerably.

Your results can depend on factors including:

- your starting weight

- the dose you can tolerate

- how long you remain on treatment

- eating patterns

- physical activity

- other health conditions and medicines

- your individual biological response

This is why percentages reported in clinical trials should not be interpreted as a guarantee of what will happen to an individual.

A healthcare professional should review whether treatment is working for you.

## Does semaglutide replace diet and exercise?

No.

Weight-management medicines are intended to support broader treatment rather than replace it.

NICE recommends that weight-management medicines are accompanied by support around nutrition, physical activity and behavioural strategies.

That does not mean someone needs to follow an extreme diet or exercise programme.

The aim is to develop changes that are realistic and sustainable alongside treatment.

## What are the common side effects?

Semaglutide commonly affects the digestive system.

Side effects can include:

- nausea

- vomiting

- diarrhoea

- constipation

- stomach discomfort

These effects are often more noticeable when treatment begins or when the dose is increased.

Eating smaller meals and following the advice provided by your healthcare team may help.

Speak to your prescriber if side effects are persistent, severe or difficult to manage.

## Are there serious risks?

Like all medicines, semaglutide can cause less common but potentially serious problems.

Seek medical advice urgently if you develop severe or persistent abdominal pain, particularly if it spreads to your back or is accompanied by vomiting.

This could require assessment for conditions such as pancreatitis.

Persistent vomiting or diarrhoea can also cause dehydration.

Your prescriber should explain symptoms that need medical assessment and whether any of your existing medical conditions affect your suitability for treatment.

## Can I take semaglutide during pregnancy?

Semaglutide is not recommended during pregnancy.

Weight-loss treatment during pregnancy is not appropriate, and semaglutide may pose risks to a developing baby.

If you are pregnant, planning a pregnancy or breastfeeding, discuss this with your healthcare professional.

If you could become pregnant, your prescriber can advise you about contraception and when semaglutide should be stopped before trying to conceive.

## What happens if I stop taking it?

Some people regain weight after stopping weight-management medicines.

Obesity is increasingly understood as a long-term condition influenced by biology, environment, behaviour and social factors rather than simply willpower.

This means maintaining weight loss may require ongoing support.

Before starting treatment, it can be helpful to discuss:

- how long treatment may continue

- what happens if treatment is stopped

- what lifestyle and behavioural support is available

- how your progress will be reviewed

## Does semaglutide have benefits beyond weight loss?

Research into GLP-1 medicines has increasingly looked at outcomes beyond body weight.

For some people with established cardiovascular disease who are overweight or living with obesity, evidence has shown that semaglutide can reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events.

In 2026, NICE published specific guidance on semaglutide for reducing major adverse cardiovascular events in certain adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity.

This does not mean semaglutide prevents heart attacks or strokes in everyone who takes it.

The benefit depends on the population being treated, and decisions about prescribing should be individualised.

## What should I ask before starting treatment?

- Why are you recommending this medicine for me?

- What benefit could I realistically expect?

- What side effects should I look out for?

- How will my treatment be monitored?

- What happens if I do not lose enough weight?

- How long might I need treatment?

- What support will I receive with diet and physical activity?

- Does semaglutide interact with any of my other medicines?

## Buying weight-loss injections online

Only use prescription medicines supplied through legitimate, regulated healthcare services.

Medicines advertised through social media, unregulated websites or unofficial sellers may be counterfeit, contaminated, incorrectly dosed or contain something different from what is advertised.

You should have an appropriate clinical assessment before being prescribed semaglutide.

## Key points

- Semaglutide is a prescription medicine that can help eligible people manage their weight.

- Wegovy and Ozempic contain semaglutide but are licensed for different purposes.

- Semaglutide works partly by reducing appetite and increasing feelings of fullness.

- It should be used as part of a wider weight-management approach rather than as a replacement for nutrition, activity and behavioural support.

- Results and side effects vary between people, so treatment needs appropriate clinical assessment and follow-up.
